About Biryani

Biryani is the ultimate symbol of celebration and culinary synthesis in Indian culture. It is not just a rice dish; it is a grand communal experience that represents royalty, regional pride, and the coming together of diverse cultures

Prepration

The preparation of an authentic layered biryani begins with marinating bone-in meat in a rich mixture of yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, lemon juice, powdered spices, and crispy fried onions, allowing it to rest for at least two hours to tenderize and absorb the flavors. Meanwhile, aged basmati rice is washed, soaked, and par-boiled in water infused with whole spices like cardamom and cinnamon until it is about 70% cooked, then immediately drained. Next, the marinated meat is sautéed and simmered in a heavy-bottomed pot until fully cooked into a thick, aromatic gravy. The final dish is built inside a heavy handi by alternating layers of the cooked meat and the par-boiled rice, with each layer generously topped with chopped mint, fresh coriander, more fried onions, a drizzle of ghee, and vibrant saffron milk. To lock in the moisture and fuse the components together, the pot is sealed tightly with a rim of wheat flour dough and cooked using the Dum technique over a low-heat griddle for 20 minutes, followed by a brief rest before breaking the seal to serve.
