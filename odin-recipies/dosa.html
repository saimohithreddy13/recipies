About Dosa

Dosa is a foundational pillar of South Indian culinary identity and a global symbol of Indian food culture. It is far more than a breakfast crepe; it represents thousands of years of fermentation history, daily family rituals, and an incredible ability to adapt across social and geographic boundaries.

Prepration

The preparation of traditional dosa begins with thoroughly washing and soaking a specific ratio of parboiled rice, raw rice, urad dal, and a teaspoon of fenugreek seeds in water for four to six hours to soften. Next, the soaked lentils and fenugreek are ground into a very smooth, frothy paste, followed by the rice, which is ground into a slightly grainy texture before both are mixed together in a large container. Rock salt is then incorporated by hand to kickstart the natural fermentation process, and the covered batter is left to rest in a warm place for eight to twelve hours until it doubles in volume and becomes light, airy, and pleasantly sour.


To cook the dosa, a heavy cast-iron griddle (tawa) is heated and seasoned with a few drops of water and oil, after which a ladleful of batter is poured onto the center and quickly swirled outward in concentric circles into a thin crepe. Finally, a drizzle of ghee or oil is ladled around the edges, and the dosa is cooked on medium heat until the bottom turns crispy and beautifully golden brown, at which point it is rolled or folded over a spiced potato filling and served immediately.
